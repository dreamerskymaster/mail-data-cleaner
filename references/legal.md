# Choosing the legal basis (verify current status with WebSearch before sending)

This is not legal advice. Laws change, so check the current status at run time and
claim only rights the user actually has.

| User lives in / company is | Letter | Basis | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| A US state with a comprehensive privacy law (CA, CT, VA, CO, UT, TX, OR, MT, IA, DE, NJ, NH, TN, MN, MD, IN, KY, RI, …) | **A** | That state's act (CA: CCPA/CPRA) | Usually 45 days (+45 extension) |
| A US state with **no** comprehensive law (e.g. MA as of late 2026, while its bill sat in conference committee) | **A**, reworded as a policy-based request | Company privacy policy; ask them to honour CCPA-style rights voluntarily | None |
| Banks, lenders, crypto exchanges, insurers (GLBA / state insurance law) | **B** | Privacy policy; acknowledge AML/GLBA retention | None, but follow up after 45 days |
| Indian company, Indian data principal | **C** | IT (SPDI) Rules 2011, r.5(7) withdrawal; r.5(9) one-month grievance window; DPDP Act 2023 s.12 erasure (Phase 3, ~May 2027 unless brought forward) | 1 month |
| EU / UK company (or an EU/UK establishment) | **D** | GDPR / UK GDPR Art. 17 and 21 | 1 month (Art. 12(3)) |

## Notes from practice
- **Connecticut (CTDPA as amended by PA 25-113, in force July 2026):** lower thresholds,
  and the 60-day cure period ended in 2024. GLBA *entities* are exempt, not just GLBA data,
  so don't cite CTDPA to a bank or crypto exchange; use letter B.
- **Nonprofit universities** are generally exempt from state acts. Unsubscribe them instead.
- **Public authorities** (e.g. a transit authority) aren't covered by consumer privacy acts.
- **Insurers and quote sites** (Liberty Mutual, Insurify): often GLBA or insurance-law exempt.
  Some only offer deletion to California residents.
- **Accounts tied to money or devices:** deletion can lose balances or lock the user out of
  hardware. Ask the user company by company.
- **Accounts the user still relies on** (e.g. the Microsoft account behind their Outlook):
  never send a deletion request. Unsubscribe only.
- A company asking for **phone, address, user ID or ID documents** is "Needs user". The
  agent never supplies them.
- **"Use our web form instead"** replies: under CTDPA-style laws a consumer may use any
  method the privacy policy lists. Reply asking them to process the email; the clock runs
  from the email date.

## Authorized agents
Several state laws allow an authorized agent to submit requests, but companies may demand
signed permission and identity verification. This playbook has the user send everything
from their own address instead, so no agent paperwork is needed.
